# Approved facts for the Raktaveda assistant

**Needs review before real use. Content to be reviewed by a doctor before real use.**
Only plain, general facts belong here. The assistant may use nothing else.

## About the assistant
- This assistant answers common, general questions about blood donation and about the Raktaveda app.
- It is not a doctor. It cannot give medical advice, diagnose anything, or talk about medicines or doses.
- It can answer in English, Hindi, Korean, Simplified Chinese and Spanish.

## What blood donation involves
- A donor registers and answers some health and lifestyle questions.
- A trained person at the blood bank does a short check before the donation (for example, the blood bank may check haemoglobin and blood pressure).
- The donation itself is done by trained staff using sterile, single-use equipment.
- After donating, donors are usually asked to rest for a few minutes and have a drink and a snack.
- Different blood groups and blood components (for example red cells, platelets and plasma) are used for different needs.

## Before and after donating
- Donors should eat and drink normally before donating, and should drink enough water.
- Donors should follow the instructions given by the blood bank staff on the day.
- If a donor feels unwell after donating, they should tell the blood bank or see a doctor.

## Eligibility
- The blood bank decides whether a person can donate. A doctor can also decide.
- The eligibility result shown in Raktaveda is only a general estimate. It is not a decision.
- Rules such as age, weight and the time needed between donations are set by national guidelines and by the blood bank.
- Anyone unsure about their own situation should ask the blood bank or a doctor.

## Using the Raktaveda app
- Raktaveda helps people find blood donors and blood banks in an emergency, and helps people who want to donate.
- Donors can create a donor profile and switch "available now" on or off. Switching it off removes the saved location.
- A donor's phone number is shown to the person who asked for blood only after the donor accepts that request.
- A donor can decline a request at any time.
- Hospitals and blood banks must be checked by an app administrator before they can use features that need a verified institution, such as chat.
- To find a nearby blood bank or hospital, use the app's search for blood banks and hospitals. If the app does not show one, contact the nearest hospital.
- A person can delete their account in the app. This asks for a one-time code and the word DELETE.

## Emergencies
- In India, the emergency number is 112.
- In any emergency, call the emergency number and go to the nearest hospital immediately.

## Official information
- The e-RaktKosh service of the Government of India (eraktkosh.mohfw.gov.in) lists blood banks.
- The National Blood Transfusion Council (NBTC) and the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare publish official blood donation guidelines.
- For questions about a person's own health, ask a doctor.
